# Seven Sisters Wear: An E-commerce Platform for Traditional Dress of North East India

---

## Abstract

North East India is home to over 200 distinct tribal communities, each possessing unique traditional attires that represent their rich cultural heritage. However, these traditional dresses remain largely inaccessible to the broader market due to limited offline distribution channels. This research paper proposes "Seven Sisters Wear," an e-commerce platform designed to bridge the gap between traditional tribal artisans and customers across India and globally. The study examines the current state of traditional dress commerce in North East India, identifies challenges in existing distribution systems, and presents a comprehensive solution utilizing modern web technologies. The proposed platform aims to preserve indigenous craftsmanship, empower local artisans, and provide a sustainable marketplace for traditional attire. This paper discusses the methodology, system architecture, features, and expected outcomes of the proposed e-commerce solution.

**Keywords:** E-commerce, North East India, Traditional Dress, Tribal Artisans, Cultural Heritage, Seven Sisters Wear

---

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

North East India comprises eight states: Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, and Tripura. These states are collectively known as the "Seven Sisters" (excluding Sikkim which is often included in the broader context). The region is renowned for its extraordinary cultural diversity, with each tribe having its own distinct language, customs, and traditional attire (Baidya, 2019).

Traditional dresses of North East India are not merely clothing items but serve as visual symbols of tribal identity, social status, and cultural significance. For instance, the **Mekhla Chador** of Assam, the **Angami** shawl of Nagaland, the **Jainsem** of Meghalaya, and the **Puan** of Mizoram are examples of exquisite traditional attires that carry deep cultural meaning (Dutta, 2021).

### 1.2 Problem Statement

Despite the richness and beauty of traditional dresses from North East India, several challenges persist:

1. **Limited Market Access:** Most traditional dresses are available only through local markets, village fairs, and craft melas, making them inaccessible to customers outside the region.

2. **Lack of Digital Presence:** Artisans and local sellers lack the technical expertise to sell products online, leaving a significant gap in the digital marketplace.

3. **Supply Chain Inefficiencies:** The absence of organized logistics and inventory management systems leads to delays and increased costs.

4. **Authentication Issues:** Customers often struggle to verify the authenticity of traditional products, leading to distrust in online purchases.

5. **Price Exploitation:** Middlemen often exploit artisans by offering low prices, reducing their profit margins.

### 1.3 Research Objectives

The primary objectives of this research are:

1. To analyze the current state of traditional dress commerce in North East India.
2. To design and propose an e-commerce platform (Seven Sisters Wear) that addresses the identified challenges.
3. To evaluate the technical architecture and feasibility of the proposed solution.
4. To assess the potential impact on local artisans and the broader economy.
5. To identify challenges and limitations in implementation.

### 1.4 Significance of the Study

This research holds significant importance in the context of:

- **Cultural Preservation:** Helping preserve dying arts by providing economic incentives to artisans.
- **Economic Empowerment:** Creating sustainable income opportunities for tribal communities.
- **Digital Inclusion:** Bringing North East India's traditional products to the global digital economy.
- **Tourism Promotion:** Supporting the region's tourism industry by making traditional attire more accessible.

### 1.5 Scope and Limitations

The study focuses on traditional dresses from the Seven Sisters states and does not cover other traditional products such as handicrafts, jewelry, or food items. The proposed solution is a web-based e-commerce platform, though mobile applications may be considered in future phases.

## 2. Literature Review

### 2.1 E-commerce in India

India's e-commerce sector has experienced exponential growth over the past decade. According to the Internet and Mobile Association of India (IAMAI, 2024), the Indian e-commerce market is projected to reach $325 billion by 2026. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated digital adoption, with more consumers turning to online shopping (Reddy, 2023).

Research by Kumar and Sharma (2022) highlights that tier-2 and tier-3 cities are driving e-commerce growth, with increasing internet penetration and smartphone usage. However, traditional and heritage products remain underrepresented in major e-commerce platforms.

### 2.2 Traditional Products and E-commerce

Several studies have examined the potential of e-commerce for traditional and handcrafted products. According to Choudhury (2020), digital platforms can significantly enhance the reach of traditional products while maintaining authenticity. The study by Singh and Patel (2021) emphasizes the importance of proper product storytelling and visual presentation in selling heritage products online.

The Government of India has launched several initiatives to promote handloom and handicraft products online, including the National Handloom Development Programme and the e-marketing platform "India Handloom Brand" (Ministry of Textiles, 2023). However, these initiatives have limited coverage of North East India's unique tribal dresses.

### 2.3 North East India: Cultural Heritage and Economic Challenges

Research by Hazarika (2019) provides a comprehensive overview of the tribal communities in North East India and their traditional attire. The study emphasizes that traditional dresses are deeply connected to the socio-cultural identity of each tribe and require special handling in terms of documentation and presentation.

According to the North East Council (NEC, 2022), the handloom and textile sector in the region employs over 2.5 million weavers and artisans. However, the sector faces challenges including lack of market access, inadequate infrastructure, and limited financial support.

Borah (2021) conducted a study on the economic potential of traditional textiles in North East India and found that proper market linkage could increase artisan income by 40-60%. The study recommends digital platforms as a viable solution to bridge the market gap.

### 2.4 Technology Solutions for Heritage Products

Recent research by Johari and Gupta (2023) discusses technical frameworks for building heritage product e-commerce platforms. The study recommends using modern web technologies like React, Laravel, and cloud infrastructure for scalable solutions. Additionally, the authors emphasize the importance of features like product authentication, virtual try-on, and regional language support.

### 2.5 Research Gap

While existing literature covers e-commerce in India and traditional products, there is limited research specifically addressing:

1. E-commerce platforms exclusively for North East Indian traditional dresses.
2. Technical architectures tailored to the unique requirements of tribal textile commerce.
3. Cultural preservation aspects in e-commerce design.

This research aims to address these gaps by proposing a comprehensive solution tailored to the specific needs of Seven Sisters Wear.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Research Design

This research adopts a mixed-methods approach combining:

1. **Literature Review:** Comprehensive analysis of existing research, reports, and publications on traditional dress commerce and e-commerce platforms.
2. **Requirements Analysis:** Identification of user requirements through analysis of existing e-commerce platforms and target audience needs.
3. **Technical Development:** Design and implementation of the proposed platform using modern software development practices.

### 3.2 Data Collection

Data has been collected from:

- **Secondary Sources:** Academic papers, government reports, industry publications, and online resources.
- **Market Analysis:** Review of existing e-commerce platforms (Amazon, Flipkart, Etsy, Craftsvilla) for traditional products.
- **User Feedback:** Informal interactions with potential users including artisans, customers, and local sellers in North East India.

### 3.3 System Development Methodology

The platform is developed using the **Agile Development Methodology**, with iterative development cycles allowing for continuous improvement based on feedback. The development follows the **Model-View-Controller (MVC)** architecture for clean separation of concerns.

## 4. System Architecture

### 4.1 Technology Stack

The Seven Sisters Wear platform is built using the following technologies:

**Frontend:**
- **Vue.js 3:** Progressive JavaScript framework for building user interfaces
- **Inertia.js:** Modern server-side rendering framework
- **Tailwind CSS:** Utility-first CSS framework for styling
- **Laravel Blade:** Template engine for server-rendered pages

**Backend:**
- **Laravel 10:** PHP framework for robust backend development
- **MySQL:** Relational database for data storage
- **Redis:** Caching for improved performance

**Infrastructure:**
- **Apache/Nginx:** Web server
- **Milesweb:** Deployment and server management

### 4.3 Database Schema

The database design includes the following key tables:

**Users Table:**
- ID, name, email, password, role (customer/admin), phone, avatar, state, tribe, created_at, updated_at

**Products Table:**
- ID, name, description, price, category_id, state_id, tribe_id, stock_quantity, images, created_at, updated_at

**Categories Table:**
- ID, name, description, parent_id, created_at, updated_at

**States Table:**
- ID, name, code, description, image, created_at, updated_at

**Tribes Table:**
- ID, name, state_id, description, created_at, updated_at

**Orders Table:**
- ID, user_id, order_number, total_amount, status, shipping_address, payment_method, created_at, updated_at

**Order Items Table:**
- ID, order_id, product_id, quantity, price, created_at, updated_at

**Reviews Table:**
- ID, product_id, user_id, rating, comment, created_at, updated_at

**Carts Table:**
- ID, user_id, product_id, quantity, created_at, updated_at

**Wishlists Table:**
- ID, user_id, product_id, created_at, updated_at

### 4.4 Key Features Implementation

1. **Multi-state and Tribe Filtering:** Products can be filtered by state and tribe, allowing customers to explore dresses from specific communities.
2. **Rich Product Descriptions:** Each product includes detailed information about the tribe, occasion, and significance of the dress.
3. **High-Quality Image Gallery:** Multiple images showcasing different angles and details of the traditional dress.
4. **Size and Customization Options:** Support for standard sizes and custom tailoring services.
5. **Authentication Badges:** Verification system for authentic tribal products.

## 6. Expected Outcomes

### 6.1 Economic Impact

1. **Increased Income for Artisans:** Direct market access will eliminate middlemen, potentially increasing artisan income by 30-50%.
2. **Employment Generation:** The platform will create jobs in logistics, packaging, customer service, and technology.
3. **Market Expansion:** North East Indian traditional dresses will reach customers across India and globally.

### 6.2 Cultural Impact

1. **Preservation of Heritage:** Economic incentives will encourage younger generations to continue traditional crafting.
2. **Cultural Awareness:** The platform will educate customers about the cultural significance of different dresses.
3. **Documentation:** Each product listing will serve as digital documentation of tribal traditions.

### 6.3 Social Impact

1. **Women Empowerment:** Majority of traditional weavers are women; increased income will improve their economic status.
2. **Rural Development:** Sustainable income from traditional crafts will reduce migration to urban areas.
3. **Digital Literacy:** Artisans will gain digital skills through platform training programs.

### 6.4 Technical Outcomes

1. **Functional E-commerce Platform:** A fully operational website with all core e-commerce features.
2. **Scalable Architecture:** System capable of handling growth in users and products.
3. **Mobile-Responsive Design:** Accessible across devices including smartphones and tablets.

## 7. Challenges and Limitations

### 7.1 Technical Challenges

1. **Internet Connectivity:** Limited internet infrastructure in some North East Indian regions may affect user experience.

### 7.2 Operational Challenges

1. **Logistics and Shipping:** Remote locations of artisans create transportation challenges.
2. **Quality Control:** Ensuring consistent quality across products from multiple artisans.
3. **Inventory Management:** Managing stock for handcrafted items that may have limited availability.
4. **Returns and Exchanges:** Handling returns for custom-sized traditional dresses.

### 7.3 Economic Challenges

1. **Initial Investment:** Significant capital required for platform development and marketing.
2. **Price Competitiveness:** Balancing fair artisan prices with competitive market pricing.
3. **Customer Trust:** Building confidence in purchasing high-value traditional products online.

### 7.4 Mitigation Strategies

| Challenge | Mitigation Strategy |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Internet Connectivity | Progressive Web App (PWA) for offline capability |
| Logistics | Partner with local courier services, establish collection centers |
| Quality Control | Quality assurance team, artisan training programs |
| Authentication | Blockchain-based product certification system |
| Customer Trust | Detailed product documentation, video demonstrations, verified reviews |

## 8. Conclusion

### 8.1 Summary of Research

This research has explored the potential of e-commerce in promoting and selling traditional dresses from North East India. The study identified significant challenges in the current market, including limited access, lack of digital presence, and exploitation by middlemen. The proposed "Seven Sisters Wear" platform presents a comprehensive solution to address these challenges through modern web technologies.

The technical architecture based on Laravel and Vue.js provides a robust foundation for a scalable e-commerce platform. The proposed features including multi-state filtering, cultural context pages, and artisan profiles are designed specifically for the unique requirements of traditional dress commerce.

### 8.2 Key Findings

1. **Market Opportunity:** There is significant untapped demand for North East Indian traditional dresses in the online marketplace.
2. **Technical Feasibility:** Modern web technologies can effectively support the required functionality.
3. **Economic Potential:** The platform can significantly improve artisan incomes while preserving cultural heritage.
4. **Implementation Challenges:** Logistics, authentication, and customer trust remain key challenges requiring careful attention.

### 8.3 Recommendations

Based on the research findings, the following recommendations are proposed:

1. **Pilot Phase:** Start with a limited number of artisans and products in one or two states before expanding.
2. **Partnerships:** Collaborate with state government handloom departments and tribal welfare organizations.
3. **Quality Assurance:** Implement rigorous quality checks and artisan verification processes.
4. **Marketing Strategy:** Focus on cultural storytelling and authenticity to differentiate from generic e-commerce.
5. **Customer Education:** Invest in content marketing to educate customers about the cultural significance of products.

### 8.4 Future Scope

The research suggests several avenues for future development:

1. **Mobile Application:** Native mobile apps for iOS and Android platforms.

2. **AI Recommendations:** Personalized product recommendations based on user preferences.

### 8.5 Final Remarks

Seven Sisters Wear represents more than just an e-commerce platform—it is a bridge between the rich cultural heritage of North East India and the global marketplace. By empowering local artisans and making traditional dresses accessible to a wider audience, this initiative contributes to cultural preservation and sustainable economic development. The success of this platform will demonstrate how technology can be leveraged to protect and promote indigenous traditions in the digital age.
